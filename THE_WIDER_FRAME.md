## Tier One: What Is Historically Documented

**Feynman at Chapel Hill, 1957, and after.** Richard Feynman approached gravitation as a quantum field theory problem. In his Lectures on Gravitation (1962-63), he treated gravity as arising from a massless spin-2 quantum field and showed that Einstein's field equations follow from Lorentz invariance plus self-consistency requirements. On that route, gravity is not an independent geometric ingredient added to physics. It is a consequence of quantum field structure.

**Feynman's phase speculation, stated precisely.** At the 1957 Chapel Hill Conference, Feynman speculated that quantum mechanics might fail at large distances and for large objects, roughly at masses near 10 to the 22 grams where gravitational self-energy becomes comparable to quantum scales. The proposed mechanism was a slight smearing in phase evolution, which would turn amplitudes into probabilities for sufficiently complex objects. He then took the speculation one conditional step further: if one consequence of that phase smearing were the existence of gravitation itself, then, in his words, "there would be no quantum theory of gravitation," an outcome he called terrifying. This is documented in the conference proceedings and analyzed in the modern historical literature. The significance for this package: the idea that macroscopic gravity might emerge from phase structure rather than stand as an independent force to be quantized was placed on the table by Feynman himself, at the founding conference of quantum gravity research, as a live conditional he could not rule out.

**The emergent gravity research program.** A modern line of work, most visibly Verlinde's entropic gravity and related information-theoretic approaches, treats macroscopic gravity as emergent from the entropy or information structure of underlying quantum systems rather than as a fundamental force. This is an active, contested, but legitimate research program within theoretical physics.

**The consequence, if any emergent picture is right.** If macroscopic gravity is emergent from quantum structure, there is no separate quantum gravity to quantize. The quantization already happened at the level of the underlying structure. The century-long search for a quantum theory of gravity would be a search for something that does not exist as an independent entity.

## Tier Three: Open Speculation

**The reflexive possibility.** If gravity is emergent from phase structure, as the emergent gravity programs propose, then the Theta-Gamma claim becomes reflexive. Gravity would not be an external agent that happens to set the stabilization tempo. The stabilization process and gravity would be the same phenomenon read at different scales. The tempo would not be set by gravity; the tempo would be gravity, expressed locally.

**What that would mean for kappa.** Under this reading, kappa stops being an arbitrary coupling between two separate things and becomes a measure of how phase coherence at small scales organizes into the macroscopic curvature we measure. A measured kappa would then be doing double duty: confirming the stabilization mechanism and constraining the emergence relation. This is speculation. It is flagged as such. But it is the kind of speculation that a measured kappa would either feed or starve.

**Why this section exists.** An experimentalist evaluating this package does not need any of this. The measurement is the measurement. But a theorist asking "why would nature do this?" deserves to know that the proposal is not orphaned. It connects to a real, documented lineage of questions about whether gravity is fundamental, running from Feynman's spin-2 program through the modern emergent gravity literature. The framework does not depend on that lineage being right. It is merely at home in it.

## Sources for the Documented Tier

- DeWitt, C. M. and Rickles, D. (eds.), *The Role of Gravitation in Physics: Report from the 1957 Chapel Hill Conference*, Max Planck Research Library for the History and Development of Knowledge, 2011. The primary record of Feynman's remarks, including the phase-smearing speculation.
- Di Mauro, M., Esposito, S., and Naddeo, A., "A roadmap for Feynman's adventures in the land of gravitation," *European Physical Journal H*, 2021 (arXiv:2102.11220). Detailed modern analysis of Feynman's Chapel Hill interventions, confirming both the spin-2 field program and the speculation connecting phase smearing to the existence of gravitation.
- Feynman, R. P., Morinigo, F. B., and Wagner, W. G., *Feynman Lectures on Gravitation*, Caltech 1962-63 (published 1995). The spin-2 field-theoretic derivation of Einstein's equations.
- Verlinde, E., "On the origin of gravity and the laws of Newton," *JHEP* 2011 (arXiv:1001.0785). Representative of the modern emergent gravity program.
