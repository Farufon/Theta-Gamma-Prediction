# The Theta-Gamma Prediction, Explained for Non-Physicists

## The Setup

You take two entangled particles. You hold one at ground level and one at the top of a tall building, or a tower, or an atom fountain. You wait. You don't measure anything yet; you just let them sit there. Then, after some time, you bring the measurement records together and look at how correlated they are.

## What Standard Physics Says (this part is established)

Two things here are measured fact, not proposal. First, gravitational time dilation is real: the higher particle's clock genuinely runs faster than the lower one's, and this has been confirmed to astonishing precision. Second, entanglement is real: the correlations between entangled particles exceed anything classical physics allows, confirmed in loophole-free experiments.

Standard quantum mechanics combines these and says: the gravitational time difference introduces a known, calculable phase shift between the particles. You can mathematically correct for it. After correction, the correlation score returns to its maximum value, 2 root 2, approximately 2.828. Always. No matter how long you wait or how far apart the particles were.

## What Theta-Gamma Says (this part is the proposal)

The framework proposes gravity does something more than bend space and slow clocks. It also modulates how fast a quantum system's possibilities crystallize into recorded facts. The particle higher up, where gravity is weaker, has its possibilities crystallize at a slightly different rate than the particle at ground level. This rate mismatch introduces a small, permanent scrambling of their phase relationship.

The key word is permanent. No correction can undo it. It is baked in by the stabilization process itself, not by environmental noise.

## The Prediction

If the framework is right, your measurement shows three regimes:

1. **Short times:** almost no deficit. The score is nearly 2.828, just as standard QM predicts.
2. **Medium times:** a growing deficit. The score drops. The longer you wait, the bigger the drop.
3. **Long times:** the deficit stops growing. It levels off at a floor.

That leveling off is the signature. Ordinary noise never does that. Ordinary decoherence keeps getting worse forever. This saturates.

## Why Height Matters

The bigger the gravitational difference between the two particles, the stronger the effect. The scaling is roughly linear in the height difference.

## How Big Is the Effect?

Honestly: probably very small. Here is the reasoning, and it is a feature of the framework, not an embarrassment. Physicists have been running exquisitely sensitive Bell tests and comparing atomic clocks across height differences for years. If this effect were large, it would already have shown up as unexplained noise. It has not. So the coupling constant, called kappa, that sets the effect's size must be small, the way most genuine corrections in physics are small. The Lamb shift and the electron's anomalous magnetic moment are tiny numbers that nonetheless revealed deep structure. The claim is not that entanglement visibly falls apart across a staircase. The claim is that a deliberate, sensitive measurement across a large gradient will find a specific curve where standard theory predicts a flat line.

## What You'd Measure

Run the experiment many times, compute a CHSH score (a standard correlation test), and plot it against time or height.

Standard QM prediction: a flat line at 2.828.
Theta-Gamma prediction: a curve that sags and then levels off.
Your data: tells you which one nature actually does.

## The Honest Asterisk

The framework does not yet derive kappa from first principles. So the simulation shows what the curve looks like, the saturation shape, but not how deep the sag will be in a real apparatus. Your measurement pins kappa down or kills the framework. A null result is not a wasted experiment; it is a published bound.

## In One Sentence

Theta-Gamma predicts a residual, non-compensable loss of entanglement coherence across gravitational gradients that grows with separation and observation time but saturates rather than decaying forever, making it distinguishable from both standard quantum mechanics and ordinary decoherence.
